MemoryAgentBench's Selective Forgetting competency\n\n**Source:** Hu, Wang & McAuley, *Evaluating Memory in LLM Agents via Incremental Multi-Turn Interactions*, arXiv:2507.05257, **ICLR 2026**. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257>\n\n### How it is measured\n\nSelective Forgetting is operationalised as **FactConsolidation**, built from **MQUAKE** counterfactual edit pairs (arXiv:2305.14795):\n\n> \"Each sentence containing information was assigned a number. For each edit pair, the sentence representing outdated information (the distractor) is given a smaller number, while the sentence representing more recent information (the one containing the answer) is given a larger number. We then concatenate these sentences into a long context in order according to their assigned numbers.\"\n\nTwo splits: **FC-SH** (single-hop, 262K avg context) and **FC-MH** (multi-hop), 100 questions each, chunk size 512. Agents are *explicitly told the rule* — \"newer facts have larger serial numbers\" — and mandated to resolve conflicts by finding the newest fact. This is a **guardrailed** test: it is as easy as the authors could make it.\n\n### Scores (Table 3, GPT-4o-mini backbone unless noted)\n\n| Agent | FC-SH | FC-MH | SF avg |\n|---|---:|---:|---:|\n| GPT-5-mini (400K long-context) | 78.0 | 28.0 | **53.0** |\n| GPT-4o (128K long-context) | 60.0 | 5.0 | 32.5 |\n| **HippoRAG-v2** | 54.0 | 5.0 | **29.5** |\n| **BM25** | 48.0 | 3.0 | **25.5** |\n| GPT-4.1-mini (1M long-context) | 36.0 | 5.0 | 20.5 |\n| Qwen3-Embedding-4B | 29.0 | 3.0 | 16.0 |\n| Text-Embedding-3-Large | 28.0 | 4.0 | 16.0 |\n| **Cognee** | 28.0 | 3.0 | **15.5** |\n| **MemGPT** | 28.0 | 3.0 | **15.5** |\n| MemoRAG | 21.0 | 7.0 | 14.0 |\n| Contriever | 18.0 | 7.0 | 12.5 |\n| Self-RAG | 19.0 | 3.0 | 11.0 |\n| **Mem0** | 18.0 | 2.0 | **10.0** |\n| GraphRAG | 14.0 | 2.0 | 8.0 |\n| MIRIX | 14.0 | 2.0 | 8.0 |\n| RAPTOR | 14.0 | 1.0 | 7.5 |\n| **Zep** | 7.0 | 3.0 | **5.0** |\n\n**The headline fact: BM25 — bag-of-words lexical matching — scores 25.5, beating Cognee, MemGPT, Mem0, MIRIX, GraphRAG, RAPTOR and Zep.** Zep, the system whose entire architectural thesis is temporal fact invalidation, scores **5.0**, dead last.\n\n### Prompting cannot fix it (Appendix K.2)\n\nThe authors ablate two explicit overwrite policies on GPT-4.1-mini:\n- Policy A (\"always prefer later\"): FC-SH 36.0 → 40.0, but **FC-MH collapses to 4.0**.\n- Policy B (conservative): **−4.5 points average**.\n\n> \"These findings serve as a sanity check that validates our core motivation: **Selective Forgetting cannot be solved by prompt engineering alone, and requires dedicated memory mechanism design.**\"\n\n### Caveat you should know\n\nFactConsolidation is **recall-shaped, not deletion-shaped**. A third party ran it and found single-hop *saturates*:\n\n> \"Single-hop saturates at 100% across all six systems and both context lengths. Both the original and the counterfactual edit appear in the haystack; top-10 substring scoring finds the gold answer for either ordering. … FactConsolidation tests 'can the system retrieve the edit?' ForgetEval — the deep dive\n\n**Source:** Dongxu Yang (DeepLethe), *Control-Plane Placement Shapes Forgetting: An Architectural Study of Agent Memory Across Thirteen System Configurations*, arXiv:2606.15903v2 (16 Jun 2026). <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15903> · harness <https://github.com/deeplethe/lethe>\n\n### 4.1 What it actually measures\n\nThe paper splits agent memory into two surfaces:\n\n> \"An agent's memory has two control surfaces: a *recall plane* that retrieves stored facts, and a *control plane* that mutates them — supersede, release, purge. … The field has saturated the recall plane and left the control plane untested.\"\n\nForgetting is decomposed into **five structural families** — these are the separate subscores you asked for:\n\n| Family | Definition (verbatim) | Maps to your four senses |\n|---|---|---|\n| **Supersession** | \"a new fact wins recall; the old fact leaves top-*k* (failure: confabulation across both)\" | **superseding a changed fact** |\n| **Decay** | \"a released fact (TTL, OTP-consumed) stays out of top-*k*\" | **dropping stale facts — but only TTL/explicit release** |\n| **Amnesia** | \"forget every fact about one entity, siblings survive — the hard part is *width control*\" | entity-scoped deletion |\n| **Purge** | \"hard-delete by identifier (GDPR Article 17); semantic similarity is the wrong primitive\" | **privacy/GDPR deletion on request** |\n| **Drift** | \"a chain of supersedes where only the latest wins and intermediates are unreachable\" | supersession chains |\n\n> ⚠️ **The single most important finding for Ori in this entire document:** ForgetEval's \"**decay**\" family is **TTL/explicit-release**, *not* activation decay. It tests whether a fact you *told* the system to release stays out of top-k. It does **not** test whether unused memories fade on an access-history curve.\n>\n> **Ori's ACT-R + Ebbinghaus decay is not measured by ForgetEval, not measured by MemoryAgentBench, and not measured by any harness I could find.** Of your four senses of \"forgetting,\" ForgetEval scores three (supersede, purge, TTL-release) and is silent on the fourth (decay by disuse) — which is precisely Ori's headline mechanism.\n\n**Scoring:** a case passes iff `must_contain ⊆ top-10 blob` **and** `must_not_contain ∩ top-10 blob = ∅`.
